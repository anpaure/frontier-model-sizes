## 3. Regression / methodology findings

All arithmetic reproduces. I re-derived, to full float precision, from raw inputs: the AA-branch K3-anchored fit, the ECI dated/no-date 40/60 blend, geometric AA×ECI pooling (Fable 3.5010T, Sol 3.4292T, GPT-5.5 2.5656T, Sonnet 1.5415T — all match `Frontier Estimates` col E); the price-smoothed centrals (M col); the horizon branch (α=log2(4.2)=2.0704, Fable date-TH 3.4610 min, Ryan-gap TH 3.7734 min, pooled 3.6138 min, prior 6.5340T; Sol TH 2.4748 min, prior 2.9837T; Opus prior 3.2033T; GPT-5.5 prior 2.0191T); the posterior mix 0.45/0.50/0.05 (Fable 4.6594T, Sol 3.0991T); and the final 50/50 log-space ensemble (Fable 4.2692T→4.3, Sol 2.8703T→2.9). The displayed result follows the documented method exactly. Findings on the method itself:

**MAJOR — The horizon branch's 50% weight rests on an out-of-domain scaling law.** The 4.2×-per-TH-doubling slope is fit on 35 *open-weight* models spanning ~1B–1000B, pooled across dense and MoE. The paper itself reports the pooled slope masks a 2.2× (dense) vs 8.1× (MoE) split. Fable/Sol are almost certainly sparse frontier systems; applying the pooled elasticity α=2.07 to them is a strong architectural assumption. Recomputing Fable's horizon prior with the MoE slope gives 9.6T; with the dense slope 4.5T (vs 6.5T pooled). That's a ±~50% swing in the branch that carries half the pre-crowd weight — the single largest unquantified model risk, and it is only flagged in prose, not propagated to intervals.

**MAJOR — Correlated evidence multiplied as if independent.** The three branches (benchmark 45%, horizon 50%, compute 5%) all run through the same K3=2.8T anchor and heavily overlapping inputs (AA scores drive both the benchmark branch and compute stage 1; release dates drive all three). Log-linear pooling with weights summing to 1 is a defensible *opinion pool*, but the workbook's own "Bayesian interpretation" framing overstates what it is. To its credit, Horizon Estimates row 19 concedes compute is "strongly correlated with AA/date," and the final sheet calls the 50/50 a "judgmental ensemble."

**MODERATE — The Fable "Ryan gap" input is a single qualitative opinion given ~25% effective pre-crowd weight.** Fable's horizon prior is the geometric mean of the date-law projection and a TH derived from one person's corrected estimate ("K3 pretrain ≈ Opus 4.5; Anthropic ~8 months ahead"). Half of the 50% horizon branch, for the headline model, is one expert's non-quantitative judgment (Horizon Laws B21/B47). It moves Fable's horizon prior up ~9% vs the date law alone; defensible as judgment, but it's a hidden second "crowd" inside the "evidence model."

**MODERATE — Same-base collapses are asymmetric and partly speculative.** Opus 4.5/4.6/4.7 THs are geometrically pooled into one base (2.561 min) on the stated theory they share a pretrain (`base:anthropic-opus-4-5plus`), and GPT-5.4/5.5 likewise (base:openai-gpt-5-shared covers GPT-5 through 5.5). These identity claims are not sourced to any disclosure in the data; if Opus 4.6/4.7 in fact used a new pretrain, the Opus row and (through the GPT×K3 chains) Sol shift materially. The workbook does label them "shared-base proxy," and the audit CSVs make the base IDs explicit — good hygiene, unproven premise.

**MODERATE — Extrapolation beyond calibration range.** K3's TH (2.4 min) is an *imputed* pretrain-equivalent (Ryan's correction maps K3→Opus 4.5's TH), not a measured K3 no-CoT TH. Fable's pooled TH (3.61 min) exceeds the highest measured frontier TH (GPT-5.5, 3.0 min), so the anchor ratio (TH/2.4)^2.07 is extrapolated ~1.5× in TH space beyond any measured frontier point, on a law fit to models ≤1.1 min (open-weight panel max, Kimi K2.5). Direction and rough size are plausible; precision is not.

**PASS — No calibration-on-targets found.** Fable/Sol do not appear in any fitting set: stage-1 compute calibration uses 31 open checkpoints, stage-2 uses 19 pre-2026 frontier rows, the AA branch is anchored to K3 (withheld from fit), and the ECI fits come from the source workbook's historical regression rows. Crowd numbers enter only at the final ensemble. Disclosed anchors (K3 2.8T, Grok 1.5T) correctly override their regression rows and are excluded from whiskers.

**PASS — Time treatment.** Date residualization uses continuous years since 2024-01-01 (stage 1) / 2024 base (stage 2) and the ECI dated branch uses years since 2023-01-01, matching the source workbook convention; serial dates in formulas (e.g. 46182 = 2026-06-09) check out.

## 4. Compute branch (the 5% / effective 2.5% branch)

Verified: stage 1 has exactly 31 rows (Compute Model rows 7–37, all exact AA↔Epoch checkpoints with compute values), stage 2 exactly 19 rows (rows 43–61, confident/likely language-frontier rows), matching the Data Audit sheet (rows 18–19). The two-anchor calibration factor √((2800/328.8)·(1500/301.2)) = 6.5126 reproduces exactly, as do the claimed diagnostics (stage-1 RMSE factor 4.72×, stage-2 2.15×, stage-1 R² 0.467).

**MAJOR (contained) — The raw compute chain is off by ~5–8.5× and is rescued by a 6.5× fudge factor.** The uncalibrated pipeline predicts K3 at 329B and Grok 4.5 at 301B against disclosed 2800B/1500B. The entire branch's scale is set by the geometric-mean anchor correction; the underlying regressions contribute only *relative* ordering with a 4.7× stage-1 residual factor. After calibration the branch still misses its own anchors by −24% (K3 2.14T vs 2.8T) and +31% (Grok 1.96T vs 1.5T). A branch this weak arguably deserves ~0% weight, not 5%; at 5%/2.5% effective its influence on Fable is ≈ (2.56/4.66)^0.05 ≈ −3%, so the damage is bounded and the workbook's own text (row 19, "weak regularizer") is honest about it. Verdict: methodologically ugly, materially immaterial.

Also verified: predicted Fable compute 1.12e25 FLOP — *below* Llama 3.1 405B's 3.8e25. This is facially implausible for a mid-2026 Anthropic frontier model and confirms stage 1's date-residual slope (−0.60/yr, i.e. compute *declining* with date at fixed AA score) is an artifact of the open-weight calibration set. The anchors absorb most of this, but the branch should be treated as decorative.

## 5. Crowd aggregation

Reproduced from the then-current anonymous forecast rows in `build_prediction_registry.py` and cross-checked against the v2.0 DOCX text: 14 respondents, 13 Fable points and 13 Sol points. Range→point uses the geometric midpoint √(L·H) throughout. Equal-weight geometric means were **Fable 3.911660561673619T, Sol 2.658296180508086T**, matching the then-current `exactCrowdCenters` values to the last float digit. The DOCX displayed 3.9/2.7 (n=13 each). Final ensemble: √(4.65937×3.91166)=4.26918→**4.3T**; √(3.09911×2.65830)=2.87025→**2.9T**. Exact and 1-decimal outputs both reproduce. Personal identities have since been removed; no name-to-ID mapping is retained.

**MODERATE — The crowd pool is not an independent instrument.** All 14 forecasts were relayed through one person on one day, 12 of 14 unattributed beyond a first name, none scored for calibration, and several plausibly anchored on circulating numbers (or on earlier versions of this very analysis). Giving this pool 50% log weight — equal to the entire evidence model — is a user-mandated choice, and the workbook says so explicitly ("uncalibrated… judgmental ensemble"). Statistically, 50% is indefensible as a posterior weight; as a requested blend it is at least honestly labeled. Sensitivity: at 25% crowd weight Fable ≈ 4.45T; at 0% it's 4.66T; the headline is robust to ±1 decimal only near the chosen weight.

**MINOR — Range semantics conflated.** Benjamin's 2–10T is an explicit 95% CI; others are unlabeled ranges; point forecasts carry no width. Treating all via midpoint-only discards stated uncertainty, and the geometric midpoint of a 95% CI is not the same estimator as the midpoint of a plain range. Effect on the mean is small (<2%).

## 8. Verdicts

**Data integrity:** STRONG PASS at the transcription/reconciliation layer. Every count, hash, duplicate-selection rule, quarantine flag, and headline number I tested reproduced exactly from raw sources — this is unusually good pipeline hygiene. A blanket "100% data correctness" claim is justified **only** for structural fidelity (nothing dropped, nothing double-counted, nothing altered). It is **not** justified for identity adjudication (manual aliases, not-in-Epoch calls, shared-base assignments) or for the truth of third-party estimates (Epoch compute values), which are assumptions, not data.

**Statistical defensibility of weights:** NOT defensible as a calibrated posterior; defensible only as a documented judgmental pool. The 50% horizon weight rests on an out-of-domain pooled elasticity with a known 4× architecture split; the 50% crowd weight is a user request applied to a correlated, unscored pool; the 5% compute weight (2.5% effective) buys a branch that can't hit its own anchors. Nothing here is hidden — the workbook's own text repeatedly concedes correlation and judgment — but no reader should treat the 50/25/9.6/9.6/3.4/2.5 decomposition as likelihood-derived. There is no dataset in this project from which those weights could be estimated; they are priors.

**My own central forecasts** (system-equivalent *total* parameters of the pretrained base, the quantity this project defines):
- **Claude Fable 5: ~3.5T central, 80% range ≈ 1.8T–7T.** The best-grounded pieces are the AA/ECI benchmark branch (3.4–3.5T, reproduced exactly, K3-anchored) and the crowd center (3.9T). I discount the horizon branch's 6.5T because the pooled-slope choice is the most aggressive of the three defensible elasticities (dense 2.2×→4.5T, pooled 4.2×→6.5T, MoE 8.1×→9.6T applied at an extrapolated TH) and because half of it is one expert's gap estimate. Blending benchmark, a dense-to-pooled-averaged horizon prior, and the crowd roughly equally gives ~3.5–3.8T.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol: ~2.8T central, 80% range ≈ 1.5T–5T.** Benchmark branch 3.34T, horizon 2.98T, crowd 2.66T are unusually concordant; my center sits between crowd and model.

**Do I endorse the displayed 4.3T Fable / 2.9T Sol?** **Qualified yes for Sol, qualified no for Fable.** Sol's 2.9T is within a few percent of anything reasonable given this evidence — endorsed. Fable's 4.3T is computed correctly under the stated method and sits inside any honest interval, but it is pulled ~20% above my central estimate by one contestable choice (pooled 4.2× elasticity at 50% weight, amplified by the Ryan-gap term). I would report Fable as "≈3.5–4.3T depending on architecture assumption, central 3.8T" rather than a bare 4.3T. As a single displayed point with the workbook's own ±3× whiskers, 4.3T is not misleading; as a headline without the elasticity caveat, it overstates precision. All displayed numbers follow the documented method; the dispute is with two upstream judgment calls, not with the computation.
